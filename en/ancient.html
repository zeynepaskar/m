Antiquity – XIX Century

Stop 2 of 10

Armour, bows, helmets and a horse burial: the military world of the steppe, from the earliest cultures to the nineteenth century.

Military traditions of the steppe

The hall presents the formation of military traditions and weapons from ancient periods through the nineteenth century. E-Museum describes archaeological materials from the Stone Age and Iron Age, while Orda.kz reports that the exhibition includes real archaeological finds and reconstructions connected with Saka, Golden Horde and Kazakh Khanate history.

The gallery is lit like a jewellery box: armoured figures stand against deep-red walls, single helmets rest on white plinths, and painted backdrops of horsemen show the landscape the objects came from.

Historical periods

  1. Ancient archaeological cultures
  2. Early steppe societies
  3. Turkic historical period
  4. Golden Horde period
  5. Kazakh Khanate
  6. XVIII–XIX centuries

Weapons and artifacts

Bow and arrow
Important long-range weapons of steppe warfare.
Sword and dagger
Close-combat weapons represented in historical collections.
Armor
Protective equipment developed in response to changing weapons and tactics.
Shield
Round shields, some painted with animals and riders.
Firearms
Long-barrelled guns of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

The horse and the steppe

Among the most striking exhibits is a horse skeleton laid out in a reconstructed burial pit. For the peoples of the steppe the horse was central to warfare, travel and status, and horse burials are among the best-known finds of steppe archaeology.
